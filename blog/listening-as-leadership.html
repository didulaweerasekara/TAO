Listening is the most underrated leadership skill

The best leaders we know talk less than you would expect — and understand more.

We train people to speak: to pitch, present and persuade. We almost never train them to listen. Yet in most organisations, the decisive advantage belongs to the person who has understood the situation best, and understanding starts with attention.

Three levels of listening

Most of us listen at the first level — waiting for a gap to speak. The second level is listening to understand: following the content, the logic and the facts. The third is listening for what is not said — the hesitation, the emphasis, the concern behind the question.

Leaders are rarely short of answers. What they lack is the patience to hear the real question.

Habits that build the skill

  • Put the device away. Attention is visible. So is its absence.
  • Summarise before you respond. “What I’m hearing is…” slows the conversation and prevents costly assumptions.
  • Ask open questions. “Tell me more about that” invites depth; “Did you try X?” invites defence.
  • Tolerate silence. The most important thing is often said after a pause.

Why it matters for trust

People do not need to agree with a decision to support it. They need to feel that their view was heard and weighed. Listening is the cheapest, fastest way to build that trust — and the one most often skipped when the calendar is full.

Try it this week: in your next one-to-one, aim to talk for less than a third of the time. Notice what you learn.
