Working across cultures: a practical guide

Five habits that help global teams turn difference into an advantage.

Most misunderstandings in international teams are not about language. They are about assumptions — how direct to be, how much to explain, what silence means, who is expected to decide. Nobody is wrong; everybody is simply operating from a different default.

1. Assume difference, not deficiency

When a colleague’s behaviour puzzles you, ask “what might make this reasonable from where they stand?” before drawing conclusions. This one question prevents more conflict than any policy.

2. Make the implicit explicit

In some cultures a hint is a clear instruction; in others it is barely noticed. Say plainly what you need, by when and why. Invite the same clarity back. It feels over-careful at first, and then it feels like relief.

3. Slow down the first conversation

Relationships come first in many business cultures, and tasks first in others. Spend real time getting to know people at the start of a collaboration — it is the fastest route to speed later.

Culture is what people do when no one is telling them what to do.

4. Rotate the inconvenient

If every meeting suits one time zone, one language and one style, the team is not global — it is regional with visitors. Rotate meeting times, share the burden of working outside hours and let different voices set the pace.

5. Agree how you will disagree

Some people voice objections openly; others express them privately, or through questions. Agree early on how disagreement will be raised and how it will be received, so that no style is punished.

None of this requires being an expert in every culture. It requires curiosity, humility and the willingness to talk about how you work together — not only what you work on.
