Presence is attention, not volume

Executive presence is less about charisma than discipline: knowing your point, steadying yourself and giving people your full attention.

Ask professionals to describe someone with executive presence and the same words come back: calm, credible, clear. Almost nobody says loud. Yet the common assumption, especially early in a career, is that presence means taking up more space.

It does not. Presence is the impression that you are fully where you are — attentive to the people in front of you, certain of what matters and unhurried by what does not. That impression is built from behaviours, and behaviours can be practised.

Decide what the room should leave with

Before a meeting or presentation, write one sentence: what should they think, feel or do differently when I finish? People with presence rarely wander, because they know where they are going. Everything they say either moves towards that point or is left out.

Use the pause

Nervous speakers rush to fill silence. Composed speakers understand that a short pause before an important point draws attention to it. Pause before answering a difficult question, too; it signals that you are thinking, not reacting.

Presence is not about being the loudest person in the room. It is about being the most attentive.

Steady yourself before you speak

Pressure shows first in the body: shallow breathing, a faster voice, a tighter jaw. Slow your exhale, plant your feet and reduce your pace a little. Listeners take their emotional cues from the speaker, so steady yourself first.

Make it about them

The people remembered as having presence are usually curious. They ask a better question, remember a detail from last time and make the other person feel heard. That attention is much of what people mean when they say someone has presence.

Practise it

  • Open your next meeting by stating its purpose in one sentence.
  • Count to two before answering the first question you are asked.
  • Ask one follow-up question before offering your own view.
